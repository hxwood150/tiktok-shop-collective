What I found

When I started, I said my algorithm was convinced it knew what I would buy, and that I didn't think it was right. After all of these captures, the answer is more complicated. It was often right about what I like: boots, cozy fall style, quarter zips, even products I had already bought. But knowing my taste is not the same as getting me to buy. Most of the time I passed because I already owned something similar, the price was too high, the video didn't show enough, or a comment talked me out of it.

What I didn't expect was how much this project would change the way I watch ads. Annotating every capture taught me to notice the tactics: repeated products in new colors, sale tags, urgency, and creators who sell the same thing over and over. It also made me wonder whether sharing these products with you is doing some of the algorithm's work for it.

Moments that stuck with me
Aug 31Where it started

The algorithm brought back a top I had removed from my saved items. It remembered and made a second attempt months later.

Oct 8What I learned

I skipped the cardigan but followed the creator instead. I know my likes, interests, and budget, and I keep discovering new things through my feed.

Oct 11The twist

If I'm a fan of a product, it turns into an ad. Even my annotations can work as ads, which may mean I'm proving the algorithm right.
